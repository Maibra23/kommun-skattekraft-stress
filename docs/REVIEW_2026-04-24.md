### 4.2 Coefficient interpretation

- **unemployment_rate:** A 1 pp within-kommun increase in unemployment is associated with a 0.059 pp decrease in tax base growth. Correct sign, highly significant.
- **dependency_ratio:** A 0.1 increase in the dependency ratio (10 more dependents per 100 working-age) is associated with a 0.375 pp decrease in tax base growth. Correct sign, highly significant.
- **population_growth_pct:** Negative after demeaning. See 7.11 - this reflects within-entity dynamics (temporary per-capita dilution from population inflows), not the between-entity positive correlation visible in raw data.
- **edu_share:** Insignificant. Education stocks move slowly within kommuner over 15 years, producing insufficient within-entity variation for precise estimation. The "no_education" robustness spec should verify that removing it doesn't materially change other coefficients.

## 5. Potential Biases

### 5.1 Simultaneity / reverse causality
Low tax base growth may **cause** unemployment (firms leave to jobs lost), not just the reverse. Two-way FE does not address this. The lagged-IV robustness spec partially mitigates by using t-1 values of X, but this is not a formal instrument.

### 5.2 Omitted variable bias (time-varying)
Entity FE absorbs time-invariant confounders and year FE absorbs common shocks, but time-varying kommun-specific confounders (e.g., a major employer relocating, a local infrastructure investment) are not controlled for. These appear in the residual and may bias beta estimates if correlated with the included X variables.

### 5.3 Survivorship / boundary stability assumption
The analysis assumes stable kommun boundaries throughout 2010-2024. While verified (no mergers/splits in this window), any future extension beyond 2024 should re-verify against SCB's boundary change register.

### 5.4 Measurement error in dependent variable
Skattekraft for year t reflects income from year t-2 (METHODOLOGY 7.6). The growth rate therefore captures changes in **two-year-lagged** income, not contemporaneous economic activity. The model's X variables (unemployment, demographics) are measured at time t, creating a timing mismatch that could attenuate coefficients.

## 7. Robustness Recommendations

### 7.1 Priority checks when robustness specs are reviewed
1. Does beta_3 (population_growth) remain negative in the "lagged" spec? If it flips to positive, the negative sign may be a simultaneity artifact.
2. Does removing edu_share ("no_education" spec) materially change beta_1, beta_2, beta_3? If not, the insignificant variable is not distorting other estimates.
3. Does the "no_covid" spec show higher R2(within)? If so, COVID years add noise that masks structural relationships.
4. Does the "large_only" spec show similar coefficients? If small kommuner have different dynamics, the full-sample estimates may be diluted.

### 7.2 Possible model improvements (out of scope for current sprint)
- Lagged dependent variable (dynamic panel / Arellano-Bond)
- Spatial lag / spatial error model (neighboring kommun spillovers)
- Population-weighted estimation
- Separate estimation for urban vs rural kommuner
